# 04 — Database and data dictionary

Last updated: 2026-11-07

**Product:** kreators.app
**Version:** 0.7, reconciled with the app on 7 November 2026
**Source of truth:** the 126 migrations in `apps/api/migrations` (0001 to 0132; the numbers 0090 to 0094 and 0107 were never used), applied in order with `pnpm db:migrate:local` or `pnpm db:migrate`. Applied to an empty database they make 230 tables (183 after 0060, 198 after 0073, 203 after 0083). This doc lists every table and its columns as they stand after migration 0113. Types are omitted; `_at` columns are UTC ISO strings, `_cents` are integer cents, `_points` are integer points, `_json` is a JSON string. A migration index is at the end. What the export and the delete do with each table is `node scripts/data-coverage.mjs --table` (section 13).

Migrations 0001 to 0128 are committed (0124 to 0128 came with "alphav2.2"); the tree since adds 0129 (`invite_active`), 0130 (`rank_place_pay_guides`), 0131 (`connect_made_as`) and 0132 (`connect_rank_guide`), not committed yet. 0110 and 0111 stay exactly as they were committed; 0122 and 0123 carry the lesson words changed since. Two of the committed ones, 0101 and 0105, rebuild `reviews` and `review_templates`, so back up first (see 09 §6).

## 1. Principles as built

- Ids are random strings from `newId()`; sessions are two UUIDs; tokens at rest are SHA-256 hashes. The tokens in a link a person shares (a client link, a team link, an invoice, an agreement, the private calendar) are long random codes kept in the clear in their own row, because the link is the only key; a fresh link replaces the old one. A closed or renewed client or team link is kept only as a hash (`retired_portal_links`). Sessions opened by a link's password (`portal_sessions`, `team_link_sessions`) are kept only as hashes.
- Every time is a UTC ISO string. Zone conversion only through `zonedToUtc` and `slotsFor` in shared. Dates that mean "a day for a person" are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, and the request says which day is the person's today (`?day=`, or a `date` in the body). Rows that count by the person's calendar keep that day beside the UTC time (`published_day` and `approved_day` on `agency_items`, `day` on `focus_rounds`, `agency_time` and `water_logs`).
- Money is integer cents with a currency (INR by default); points are integers; invoice GST and TDS are basis points (one percent is 100). Never floats.
- Statuses are constrained in code by `oneOf` against the lists in shared, not by database enums.
- Nothing financial is overwritten: `points_ledger`, `credit_ledger`, `xp_events`, `orders`, `webhook_events`, `audit_logs`, `timeline_events`, `social_metrics` and `campaign_events` are append-only histories, and so are `terms_acceptances` and `consent_records` (migration 0059); a void or a refund writes the reverse.
- Sensitive data is separate and restricted: journal, wellbeing, Companion, Discover and the water and reminder rows are read only by their owner; KYC keeps a hash and the last four digits; positions are rounded to three decimals and never returned; a chat partner's id never reaches the browser; an anonymous post or reply keeps the author's id for staff and shows everyone else a made-up name.
- Owner-scoped tables carry `user_id` or `owner_id` and every query filters by the session user. Section 12 says, group by group, who can read what, what the export holds, and what erase and delete do.
- The pyramid, the tasks and the sums that feed Reviews and Life are computed from these rows; nothing is stored twice except where a saved review keeps a snapshot of its numbers so a changed template or new data never rewrites it. Emerging (the patterns, and good and bad habits) is computed on read from these rows and stored nowhere.

## 2. Core and the game

- `users`: id, display_name, creator_type, xp, points, is_pro, leaderboard_opt_in, role, email (unique), phone, birth_date (both asked at sign-up, migration 0026; never shown to anyone else), password_hash, password_salt, handle (unique, `user_xxxxxx` until changed), friend_code (unique, 8 characters), bio, modules (JSON of the person's own switches), reminder_time, referral_code (unique), referred_by, permissions (JSON list), skills, categories, goals, experience_level, availability, location, open_to_intros, interests, identities (JSON list of identity tags, migration 0030), intake_json (the setup chat's goals and what gets in the way), getting_started_hidden_at, city, area, lat, lng, timezone, avatar_url, socials_json, public_json, links_json, fee_bps, streak, best_streak, last_checkin, onboarded_at, created_at, currency (the person's money currency, INR unless changed, migration 0070), app_prefs_json (which app groups start open and the order of the apps inside a group, migration 0074; NULL means the defaults; read only through `parseAppPrefs`). Migration 0019 dropped `email_verified_at` with the magic links. There is no separate profile or creator-profile table; the columns live here.
- `sessions`: token, user_id, created_at. At most three per person (`MAX_DEVICES`); the fourth sign-in signs the oldest out. `session_evictions` (migration 0051): token, user_id, created_at, so a device that was pushed out can be told why; the cron clears rows older than thirty days.
- `password_resets`: id, user_id, token_hash, created_at, expires_at, used_at.

- `assessments` (migration 0029): id, user_id, kind, fears_json, insecurities_json, scores_json, real_day_json, focus_json, created_at, away_json (pillars set aside as not part of life right now, migration 0066). One per session run; the latest is the one that counts.
- `placements`: id, assessment_id, user_id, relationship (unique with the assessment), core_level, floor_level, core_band, floor_band, evidence, created_at. Levels are map values from 20 to 540; the flags (capped, required, volatile) are not stored but computed by `placementFlags` whenever they are read.
- `routines`: id, user_id, assessment_id, ramp_step, general_json, exceptional_json, practices_json, created_at.

- `call_requests` (migration 0100): id, user_id, status (`LIVE_CALL_STATUSES`: asked, paying, paid, booked, done, cancelled), amount_cents, currency, provider, provider_order_id, provider_payment_id, created_at, paid_at, updated_at. An ask for the live call from the strategy session. A partial unique index keeps one open ask per person, and another index finds a row by its provider order. It becomes `paid` only in the webhook. An unpaid ask goes with the account; a paid one stays for the books without who it was for. An erase leaves it.
- **Added to `users` since 0083:** `time_blocks_seeded_at` (migration 0089: empty, the person has changed no block or template and the starting set is read from code; set, it is their rows, written once in the batch of their first change, since 2026-11-07) and `date_prefs_json` (migration 0109: the person's date and time format; NULL means the default; read only through `parseDatePrefs`, checked by `datePrefsProblem`; on `GET /me`, in the export, gone with the account, untouched by an erase).
- `xp_events`: id, user_id, relationship, source, source_id, amount, created_at.
- `spark_completions`: user_id, date, relationship, created_at. The small act.
- `honors`: user_id, honor_key, note, awarded_at. `badge_grants` (migration 0045): user_id, badge_key (primary key together), note, awarded_at. Badges a super admin gave by hand, on top of the ones `computeBadges` counts; they count for unlocks and the leaderboard and every grant is audited. `badge_awards` (migration 0065): user_id, badge_key (primary key together), earned_at (empty for a badge held before dates were kept). Written by `badgeState` the first time a badge is seen (`INSERT OR IGNORE`), so a falling count never takes it back; the counts come from `getStats`, one query, which never opens what a person wrote. Owner only, in the export, cleared by erase and delete.

- `not_to_do`: id, user_id, title, relationship, streak, best_streak, created_at. `not_to_do_days`: item_id, user_id, day, status (kept, slipped), created_at.
- `notifications`: id, user_id, kind, title, body, link, read_at, created_at, archived_at (migration 0035: archiving marks read; only an archived note can be deleted). A reminder's id is fixed (`task-<id>-<day>`, `review-<id>-<day>`, `focus-<user>-<day>`, `supp-<user>-<time>-<day>`, `agency-due-<user>-<day>`), so it is sent once. Water sends no note since 2026-11-07, and migration 0112 deleted the stored ones. Every note is written by one statement, `noteStmt` in `apps/api/src/notes.ts`, whose kind is one of `INBOX_KINDS`. The water line is rewritten in place each time, and the cron deletes it once that day is over.
- `support_tickets`: id, user_id, subject, body, status, reply, replied_by, created_at, updated_at.
- `rate_limits`: key, window_start, count. The key is `<limit name>:<address or person>`.
- `module_settings`: key, enabled, min_role, rollout. `flag_overrides`: id, flag_key, subject_type (user, role, organization), subject_id, enabled, expires_at, reason, created_by, created_at.
- `audit_logs`: id, actor_user_id, action, resource_type, resource_id, before_summary, after_summary, created_at. No IP.
- `app_logs` (migration 0046): id, at, level, area, method, path, status, ms, actor_id, actor_name, message. One line per change anyone makes, per refusal, per failed request, per cron run that did something and per error a browser reports. Never a body or a query string; ids in the path are replaced; the person's own areas (journal, wellbeing, Companion, Discover, assistant) leave only a bare server-error line with no person and no path. Only a super admin reads it. The cron deletes lines older than thirty days, and a deleted account's name is wiped from them.
- `analytics_events`: id, user_id, name (one of `TRACKED_EVENTS`), props_json, created_at. Since 2026-11-05 a person's rows go when the account is deleted, and the export carries them as `productEvents`; there is still no purge by age.
- `timeline_events`: id, entity (order, booking, user, kyc), entity_id, type, data_json, created_at.
- `email_outbox`: id, user_id, to_email, subject, body, status, error, created_at, sent_at. Every mail, sent or not.
- `webhook_events`: id (the provider's event id, or a hash of the raw body), provider, type, payload (raw, up to 20,000 characters), processed_at, error, received_at.
- `webhook_events.payload` is blanked (set to an empty string) by the cron after 365 days; the row stays as the record that the event was processed.
- **Legal and privacy (migration 0059).** `terms_acceptances`: id, user_id, doc, version, how (`signup` or `notice`), accepted_at; append-only, one row per document and version (a repeat for the same version adds nothing); an index on (user_id, doc, accepted_at). `user_consents`: user_id, purpose (`attribution`, `embeds`, `marketing`), granted, banner_version, updated_at; primary key (user_id, purpose); one row per purpose, changed from Profile. `consent_records`: id, user_id, purpose, granted, source (`popup`, `profile` or `unsubscribe`), banner_version, created_at; an append-only log of every first answer, change or answer at a newer wording, so a choice can be shown later. `data_requests`: id, kind (`export` or `delete`), requested_at, completed_at, outcome; no user id and no name, since the account is gone by then. `copyright_notices`: id, kind (`notice` or counter-notice), name, email, work, urls, statement, signature, status (`new` until staff act), note, created_at, resolved_at; sent through the public form at `/copyright`. All five are owner or staff only; the three consent and terms tables cascade with the user.
- **Real-user web vitals (migration 0060).** `web_vitals`: id, route (a route pattern with ids removed), device (phone or desktop), conn (a short connection label), lcp_ms, inp_ms, cls_milli (CLS in thousandths: 0.1 is 100), ttfb_ms, created_at. No user id, no address, no user agent, no cookie. A metric the browser did not report is NULL, never zero. Written by `POST /api/public/vitals` (limit `vitals`), read in summary by staff at `GET /api/internal/vitals` (the 75th percentile per route and device), deleted after 90 days by the cron (`pruneVitals`).

## 3. Daily: tasks, focus, reminders and the calendar

- `nodes`: id, user_id, kind (pillar, principle, value_goal, outcome, project, task), parent_id, relationship, title, note, status, xp, due_date, due_time, estimate_min, ready_at, completed_at, sort_order, created_at, updated_at, and, for tasks, added_by (a life coach's user id when a coach made it, migration 0034), do_date (the day to do it; the only date that holds a tick back, migration 0048), repeat_json (a `Repeat` rule: daily, weekdays, weekly on chosen weekdays, monthly, yearly, every N, an optional end day. For monthly and yearly rules it can also hold `anchor`, the first day the task was on, set by `spawnNext` so a task that fell on the 28th of a short month goes back to the 31st; it lives inside this JSON, so there is no column and no migration for it. `parseRepeat` drops an `until` or an `anchor` that is not a real calendar day), spawned_from (the task a repeat came from, so unticking takes the untouched child back), priority (0 to 3 for P0 to P3, null for none, migration 0049), remind (0 or 1: the Remember tag), origin (`build` for tasks the strategy session or the vibe check made from the pillars), for_review (0 or 1: brought to the review for its period, migration 0057), habit (NULL keeps the old rule, where a repeating task wears the Habit tag; 1 or 0 is the person's own pick, carried to the next one by `spawnNext`, migration 0082). The pyramid is a tree of nodes.
- `subtasks` (migration 0049): id, user_id, task_id (cascades with the task), title, done_at, sort_order, created_at. A small checklist that pays nothing, up to twenty a task.
- `focus_rounds` (migration 0047): id, user_id, node_id, title (kept so the history survives the task being deleted), minutes, day, created_at, quality (1 to 5, or null; how focused the person said they were, migration 0056), mode (timer, pomodoro, stopwatch, or manual for a session added afterwards), log_id (the mindful `wellbeing_logs` row written in the same batch, so a change or a delete moves it too) and client_ref (the browser's name for one part, unique per person, so a duplicated tab never logs a part twice), all three from migration 0073. Owner only; feeds the focus dashboard and the Today list on Mindfulness.
- `focus_prefs` (migration 0053): user_id, settings_json, updated_at. One row per person: the timer lengths and counts, what counts as mindful minutes, the sound switch, the break beats, the buzz, the background sound and its volume. Read and written through `parseFocusSettings`.
- `reminder_prefs` (migration 0039): user_id, water_on, water_goal_ml, water_glass_ml, water_every_min, water_from, water_to, last_water_at, updated_at. Since water notes ended on 2026-11-07, `water_on`, `water_every_min`, `water_from`, `water_to` and `last_water_at` are columns nothing reads or writes; only the goal and the glass are used. No column was dropped. `water_logs`: id, user_id, day, ml, created_at. `custom_reminders`: id, user_id, title, time, days_json, enabled, last_sent_day, created_at. Owner only; water pays no xp. The reminders screen is computed, not stored: `GET /reminders` answers with the water state, the person's own reminders and `tasks`, the open tasks tagged Remember (`RememberedTask`: id, title, day = the do date or else the due date, time = `due_time`). Those come straight from `nodes` (`remind = 1`), so the Water and reminders box, the Daily tag and the inbox note are one thing and there is no table of remembered tasks.
- `day_types` (migration 0077): user_id, day (primary key together), kind (one of `DAY_TYPE_KEYS`), created_at, template_id (migration 0089: the day template set for that date). The kind of day, set for a date. An older row finds its template by `day_templates.source_kind`. It only changes how Daily lays out and sorts tasks, and pays nothing. Owner only, in the export, deleted with the account; an erase leaves it.
- **Time blocks (migration 0089, design in `docs/time-blocks.md`).** A person has rows here only from their first change to a block or a template; until then the starting set is read from code, with the same ids (2026-11-07). `time_blocks`: id, user_id, seed (the starting block's key, null for the person's own), name, tag, color (a swatch key from `TIME_BLOCK_SWATCHES`, or the person's own hex), life_area, relationship, sort, created_at, updated_at. `day_templates`: id, user_id, seed, source_kind (the old `DAY_TYPES` key it was made from), name, lead and second (pillar keys), why, source, is_default (one per person, by a partial unique index), sort, created_at, updated_at, color (migration 0098: the kind's own colour, read by `templateColor`). `day_template_slots`: id, template_id, user_id, block_id, start_min, end_min (0 to 1439, never equal; a slot whose end is before its start runs past midnight). `week_patterns`: user_id, weekday (1 to 7; primary key together), template_id, updated_at. These are settings: owner only, in the export, gone with the account, kept through an erase.
- **Added to `nodes` since 0082:** `life_area` (migration 0085: an optional area of life, NULL for none, read by `parseLifeArea`; it never changes what a tick pays), `time_block_id` (migration 0089: the block the person picked for the task, with a partial index) and `open_kind` (migration 0120: Close the unfinished, one of `unfinished`, `unresolved` or `undecided`, NULL for none, read by `parseOpenKind`; a tag and a view that never changes what a tick pays, carried by `spawnNext`. Letting one go is the existing `status = 'archived'`, so no new state; Bring back sets it active).
- `calendar_feeds` (migration 0056): user_id, token (unique, 24 characters), enabled, created_at. The person's private calendar link. It exposes only a title, a pillar and a priority for each open task that has a day (and the time when it has one), and the person's scheduled reviews; never a note, the journal, a mood or a tracker.

## 14. Departures from the v0.1 model

- No `user_profiles`, `creator_profiles`, `roles` or `user_roles`: the columns are on `users`; roles are one column and permissions a JSON list.
- No `feature_flags` table with environments: `module_settings` is keyed by module key, plus `flag_overrides`.
- No `achievements` or `user_achievements`: badge rules are data in shared (`BADGES`, one count reaching a number), the counts come from `getStats` in `db.ts`, and an earned badge is written once to `badge_awards` (migration 0065) by `badgeState`. Honors and by-hand badges are stored in `honors` and `badge_grants`. The leaderboard still counts its badges with `computeBadges` in `db.ts`.
- No `payment_transactions`, `ledger_entries` or `payout_requests`: `orders` carries the provider references, `points_ledger` is the points history, payouts of content rewards are rows on `bounty_submissions` plus the ledger, and bank payouts are a KYC profile reviewed by an admin.
- `knowledge_sources`, `ai_interactions` and `wellbeing_challenges` do not exist: no corpus, Companion messages are plain rows, and the assistant keeps tool names and ids only.
- Well over a hundred tables exist that v0.1 never named: the game, wellbeing, the shop, growth, finance, the store, circles, bond, run, reviews, not-to-do, the assistant, coaching, Discover, card games, media, channels, discount codes, Agency and its team.
- There is no table for Life, Home, Search, the leaderboard or Emerging: they are computed. Emerging (`GET /insights`) answers the person's whole list in one request, which the browser keeps once per device day; the server keeps nothing.

## 15. Retention and deletion

- How the delete runs (2026-11-07): `deleteAccount` goes fifty statements to a round trip through `inBatches`, each safe to run twice, with the sessions and the account row last, so a run that stops halfway can be asked for again. It first closes what others wait on (`closeWhatOthersWaitOn`).
- Delete (needs the word DELETE; an admin can do the same for a member from People, never for an admin or for themselves): `users` is anonymised (name becomes "Deleted member"; email, phone, date of birth, password, handle, friend code, bio, avatar, socials, skills, categories, goals, interests, identity tags, intake, city, area, position, location, reminder, intro opt-in, leaderboard opt-in, permissions and app groups and order cleared). Deleted: sessions and evictions, the person's `analytics_events` and the `page_events` on their page (since 2026-11-05), the kind of day they picked, nodes and subtasks, not-to-do, reviews, templates and schedules, water and reminders, focus rounds and settings, the calendar link, every Agency table and every business metric (the invoices stay for the books, scrubbed as in section 13), every Agency team row where they are the owner or the member (their name is taken off what they did on other people's Agencies), their clients' link sessions, team link sessions, retired links and password asks, their discount-code redemptions, pre-owned listings and offers, run entries and memberships, wellbeing logs, journal entries, companion messages, notifications, portfolio, KYC, the strategy session's rows, the content strategist's folders, what the assistant did, attendee claims, card unlocks, badge grants and badge awards, Discover rows, the person's side of coaching, post follows and reply reactions, event reminders, uploaded files and the media they added, their event lines and networking choices, their channel lines and seats (and the private channels they own), their run discussion lines and reads, their friendships and Club memberships, their blocks and mutes, and (since the legal pass) their posts and replies, Bond, circle and pre-owned listing messages, support tickets, Companion chat, finance dues, goals, recurring entries and budgets, the mail kept for them in the outbox (by id or address), and their terms, privacy-choice and choice-log rows. Hosted run clubs are closed, the person's products are archived, and a seat at an event still to come is cancelled (a seat at one that is over stays for the host's records). Orders, finance transactions, ledger rows (points and credits) and audit rows stay for the books.
- Erase ("start over", `POST /me/reset`): clears progress only, as in section 13. Never touched: Agency and every invoice, orders, a plan and its credits, unlocked card games, attendee claims, run entries, a life coach's links and notes, the journal and gratitude, wellbeing and Fitness logs, supplements, water and reminders, time blocks and the kind of day, review templates and schedules, business metrics, the calendar link, the app groups and order, and the Agency team.
- Export: one JSON file with everything the person wrote or that is about them, table by table, as `scripts/data-coverage.mjs` lists it, with mail by subject only and clicks as counts.
- Purges that exist (the cron): `app_logs` and `session_evictions` after thirty days, `web_vitals` after 90 days (`pruneVitals`), and the daily `tidyOld` from `RETENTION_DAYS` in shared: `email_outbox` after 90 days, spent or expired `password_resets` after 7 days, the raw text of `webhook_events.payload` after 365 days (the row stays), `link_clicks` after 396 days, `channel_messages` and `run_messages` after 365 days (`channelLines`), `cohort_talk` a whole thread at a time 365 days after its newest line, `rate_limits` windows after 2 days (`rateLimits`), notes written to a deleted account afterwards, closed `agency_password_requests` after 90 days, and `data_requests` and settled `copyright_notices` after 1,095 days. The cron also drops `portal_sessions` and `team_link_sessions` past their twelve hours, `retired_portal_links` and `retired_coach_invites` after a year, and supplement reminders from a day that is over. Also `assistant_runs` rows live only while a message runs, and rate-limit rows reset by window. The same list is shown to people as a table on `/privacy` (`RETENTION` in shared).
- Purges that do not exist: `analytics_events` and `page_events` have no purge by age. They go only when the account is deleted.
- Anything a brand, staff member, the leaderboard or Club selection can read never includes journal, wellbeing or Discover rows.

## Known gaps

- No purge by age for `analytics_events` or `page_events`; they go with the account.
- Every migration from 0001 to 0128 is in git. The work since "alphav2.2" adds 0129 (`invite_active`), 0130 (`rank_place_pay_guides`), 0131 (`connect_made_as`) and 0132 (`connect_rank_guide`), which are not added to git yet (`??`), with three other new files. Commit with `git add -A` before a deploy.
- A restore procedure is written (`docs/security/RESTORE.md`) but has not been rehearsed.
- `circle_members.revealed` is always 0 and could go.
- `reminder_prefs.water_on`, `water_every_min`, `water_from`, `water_to` and `last_water_at` are read by nothing since 2026-11-07. They could go in a later migration.
- Erase leaves a coach's links and notes by design; a person who wants them gone must end the link or delete the account.
- Savings goals in a review are shown as they stand today, because the app keeps no dated record of each amount added. A small dated table would fix it; not built, for the founder to decide.
- Media uploads can never be deleted by their owner, only with the account (a person is capped at 60).
- Left by the delete on purpose (the founder may decide otherwise): a note already in another person's inbox keeps its words, since it names no sender; a help ticket made from a report quotes the reported line and stays with the reporter; `circle_groups.plan` and `venue` have no author recorded, so they stay; `bond_chats` stays for the other person, with made-up names only; a seller's paid and undelivered work is told, not refunded by itself (an admin refunds from Admin, Orders); and no inbox note has a time limit.

## 2026-11-03 additions
