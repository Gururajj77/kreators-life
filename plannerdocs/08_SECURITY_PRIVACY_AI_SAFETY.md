## 1. Security baseline as built

**Sign-in is hand-built** on Hono and D1, with no package. That departs from the v0.1 advice to use a maintained provider. The reason, decided on 23 October 2026, is the anonymous device session that an email and password are layered onto later. The rules:

- Session tokens are two UUIDs in `sessions`, sent as a Bearer header. An account can be signed in on at most three devices at once (`MAX_DEVICES`). A fourth sign-in signs the oldest out (`newSession` is the one place a session is made). The pushed-out token is kept for thirty days in `session_evictions` (the cron clears it, and it goes with the account). So that device is told why (`{ error: 'unauthorized', reason: 'devices' }`) instead of a bare 401.
- Passwords are PBKDF2-SHA256 at 100,000 iterations, the Workers free-plan ceiling, with a 16-byte hex salt and a constant-time compare.
- One password rule, `passwordProblem`, runs on the forms and on both endpoints.
- Sign-in answers the same for an unknown email and a wrong password. For an unknown email it hashes against a decoy salt, so the timing matches.
- Changing a password needs the current one and signs out every other device (`POST /me/password`). The current password is checked with the same constant-time compare as sign-in, and the route has its own limit (`passwordChange`, five in fifteen minutes).
- A reset link is 32 random bytes, stored as a SHA-256 hash, good for thirty minutes, one open per account. Any email gets the same answer. Using the link signs every device out. The link is built from the `PUBLIC_URL` variable, never from the request's Host header (see §14).
- Founder addresses in `admins.ts` and `ADMIN_EMAILS` become admins on sign-up or sign-in, audited. Until mail verifies addresses, whoever signs up with one first gets it. Sign up with them right after a deploy.
- Sign-up also asks for a phone number and a date of birth. The server refuses anyone under 18 (`MIN_AGE`). A phone without a country code is read as Indian (`normalizePhone`: a bare ten-digit number from 6 to 9, or one with a leading 0, gets +91; one typed with a plus is kept). Neither is ever shown to anyone else. The date of birth cannot be changed afterwards.

The rest of the baseline:

- Roles, permissions, ownership and module flags are checked on the server on every route. See 05.
- Secrets are Worker bindings set with `pnpm cf:secret`. Locally they sit in `.dev.vars`, which git ignores. No key is in the repo or the bundle.
- Every input passes through `str`, `num` or `oneOf` with bounds. Every money amount is an integer. Picture and link blocks are https only. The Markdown renderer emits no HTML and no links. Nothing uses `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`.
- Rate limits live in one file, `apps/api/src/limits.ts`. `LIMITS` holds every name with its count and window. `ROUTE_LIMITS` says which method and path each one guards, and `index.ts` wires them. The doors without a session (paths under `/auth/`, `/public/` and `/admin/`) are counted per address before the session check; everything else per person after it. A change to a number is made there and nowhere else. There are 63 names (05 §13 lists the ones added since 5 November):
  - **Per address:** `signin` 10 a minute, `signup` 10 an hour, `forgot` 10 an hour, `reset` 20 an hour, `anon` 60 an hour, `lead` 5 a minute, `clientError` 20 in ten minutes, `vitals` 60 an hour, `notice` 5 an hour, `unsubscribe` 30 an hour, `portal` 30 in ten minutes (a client approving, sending back, commenting, accepting, choosing a retainer or filling the onboarding form), `portalLogin` 5 in ten minutes (the password on a client link or paper), `portalPasswordAsk` 3 an hour, `teamLinkLogin` 5 in ten minutes, `discountAddress` 60 in ten minutes, `adminKey` 10 in ten minutes, `coachJoin` 60 an hour, `onboardingSave` and `contractAnswers` 300 in ten minutes each, and `onboardingSubmit` 10 in ten minutes.
  - **Per email:** `signinEmail` 20 tries a quarter hour and `forgotEmail` 3 reset mails an hour.
  - **Per client link, whatever the address:** `portalLoginLink` and `teamLinkLoginLink`, 20 tries in ten minutes each. **Per coach:** `coachJoinLink`, 300 an hour.
  - **Per person, an hour unless said:** `checkout` 10 a minute; `post` 10, `reply` 30, `like` 120, `follow` 60, `message` 120, `channelMessage` 180, `intro` 20, `offer` 20, `create` 30, `ticket` 5, `apply` 10, `entry` 10, `claim` 10 (attendee codes, so a custom word cannot be guessed at), `research` 40, `generate` 20, `setup` 20, `reminder` 240, `upload` 30, `assistant` 60, `feelings` 30, `companion` 60, `coachAsk` 20, `coachWrite` 60, `coachRead` 300, `coachLookup` 120, `discover` 30, `prefs` 240, `gratitude` 240, `insights` 240, `agency` 600, `lifecycle` 120, `docEmail` 10, `passwordChange` 5 in fifteen minutes, `discount` 20 in ten minutes, `teamInvite` 20 a day, and `programInvite` and `courseInvite` 100 a day.
  - Four plain reads have a limit, since each rebuilds many rows: `GET /insights`, `GET /insights/dashboard`, and a coach's report and patterns (`coachRead`). The coach's lookup (`coachLookup`) is held so a box cannot be used to walk the people list. A checkout, enrolment or RSVP spends the discount budget only when it carries a code.
- **The shared admin key's door (2026-11-07).** `ADMIN_KEY` on `/api/admin` has limit `adminKey`: ten tries in ten minutes per address, on every method of `/admin/*`, through `adminKeyDoor` in `ops.ts`, the one compare. Each try takes a place before the compare, so a burst cannot get more than ten compared, and a right key gives its place back. Both sides are hashed before `safeEqual`, so neither length nor content shows. Once the tries are spent even the right key answers 429, or a good guess would still show. An admin's own session is never counted, even beside an old key on a device. A method with no door (OPTIONS) opens nothing. It is per address, so a long random key stays the real defence.
- **Links people type for others to open (widened 2026-11-07).** A meeting, a replay, a video, an event, a product's file, a portfolio piece, a School hand-in and Army work all go through `httpUrl` in `db.ts`: http or https only. `badLink` and `LINK_PROBLEM` refuse a bad one with a 400, so nobody loses what they typed. Rows written before stay as they are, behind React and the content policy. A lesson's resources take https or `/resources/` (`parseLessonResources`).
- `secureHeaders()` runs on every API answer. There is no CORS: the app and the API are one origin, and the old `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` was removed on 2026-11-01 (§14).
- A switched-off module is refused by the server, not only hidden. `MODULE_FOR_PREFIX` and `modulesForPath` in `access.ts` map every API prefix to its module. The middleware in `index.ts` answers 403 when none of its modules is available to the person (the global flag, an override, the rollout and the minimum role all count). A new route file gets a line in that map, or it is treated as core and always open.
- CSRF barely applies: sessions are Bearer tokens, never cookies. As defence in depth, a state-changing request that carries a foreign `Origin` is refused with 403 `wrong origin` (§14).
- Uploads are small and checked. A picture or a GIF (JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF or GIF) is up to 1 MB (`MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES`; the browser shrinks pictures first). It is accepted only when its first bytes say what its header says. It is kept as base64 in D1 (`media_files`) and served from `/api/public/media/:id` only as the type it really is. A person keeps at most 60 files (`MAX_FILES_PER_PERSON`), and `upload` limits it to 30 an hour. A person's files go with their account. A video is only ever a link (YouTube and Vimeo in their own player, or a direct .mp4 or .webm). Uploading to R2 for video is not built. Every deliverable and portfolio item is still a link.
- Every query is a prepared statement with bound values.
- Cloudflare gives TLS in transit and D1 encrypts at rest.
- A 500 logs the error and answers one plain line with a `requestId` (the app's own log keeps the message, never a body). Audit rows carry summaries, never passwords or journal text, and no address.
- No clickable links in what people write to each other. Posts, replies, chat lines (Bond, circles, channels, event conversations, run discussions, pre-owned listings), coach notes, inbox lines and the notes inside Agency render through `components/Inert.tsx`. It keeps a web address as plain text, never an anchor. `index.html` tells phones not to turn numbers, emails or addresses into links. `scripts/scan.sh` fails on any `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`. The only real links are the ones the owner sets on purpose: a public page's link blocks, an event host's photo-folder link (from a short list of hosts), a deliverable's https link, and partner links.
- Webhooks verify the signature on the raw body, store the raw payload in `webhook_events`, then process; a duplicate is a no-op. A money order becomes `paid` only in the webhook after the signature checks out. Payment code talks to `apps/api/src/payments.ts`, never to a provider SDK directly.

## 2. Who sees what

- A brand member sees only the campaigns and listings of their own organizations. Anyone else gets "not your organization".
- A person sees only their own applications, submissions, orders, bookings, finance, journal, wellbeing, reviews, not to do items, Discover answers, Agency records, templates and scheduled reviews. Someone else's row answers 404. Every owner-scoped query filters by the session user id.
- A learner sees a cohort only when enrolled, or with the mentor permission.
- A host sees the registrants of an event. Other attendees see only their own registration and the feedback average. A circle's host sees counts, never who.
- Staff see only the queues their permissions open (`can(user, permission)`). A permission on a plain user is refused. An admin holds every permission except Life coach, which is given on purpose. A guide in Community is anyone holding the moderation permission (`isGuide`, through `can`): an admin, a sub admin given it, or a creator given it (5 November), one rule for posts and channels.
- **Journal and wellbeing** never reach brands, staff, the leaderboard or Club selection. They reach a model only for their owner, the journal only when the owner asks about it, and only through a model that keeps no data. The one other door is a life coach the person said yes to. Wellbeing numbers (mood, sleep, stress, mindful minutes, movement and the eating number, never a tracker's note) and saved reviews (never their snapshot) reach that coach only while the person's own Reflection switch on that link is on. The journal and gratitude never reach a coach, in any form, whatever is switched on.
- **Fitness and supplements.** Movement and the eating check-in are `wellbeing_logs` rows, so the journal rule above holds. They are kept out of Logs, and a coach reads minutes, days, kinds and the eating number only under Reflection, never a note, a distance or an effort. The assistant reads them through `get_fitness`, marked sensitive, only with a model that keeps nothing. **Supplements** are stricter: owner only on every statement, with no reader but the owner. They never reach Emerging, the brief, the leaderboard, Club, a coach (not even under Reflection), the assistant or Companion, search, the calendar link or the public page. Reviews shows its owner one count, never a name, kept in that review's own snapshot, which a coach never gets. Switching Track supplements on is the consent; `/privacy` names the ground (explicit consent for health data under GDPR Art. 9(2)(a) for the EU and UK).
- **Gratitude.** Journal data: owner only, never brands, staff, the leaderboard, Club or a coach. The assistant reads it only through `read_journal` (sensitive, a model that keeps nothing, only when asked about the journal). Only a count of days leaves it, for Reviews and the Journal's By period tile.
- **Discover** (quizzes, add-ons, Human design, numerology) is owner only like the journal: never shown to brands, staff, the leaderboard or a coach. Only a short profile can reach the assistant: the top and quietest strength, the trait bands and the reasoning level. For the add-ons it is the four letters, the top love languages and, with their own switch, the three mind bands. It goes only while the owner's assistant switch is on and only to a model that keeps no data. Birth details stay on the person's own table. The answer keys for the situations and the emotional reading live on the server (`discoverKeys.ts`), so the web page never carries the best answers.
- **Emerging** (patterns across a person's own apps, `GET /insights`) is core, owner only and read only. It is computed on read and stored nowhere. A pattern runs only while every app it reads is on, and the reads for an app that is off are never sent. The journal is read only by when an entry was written, never a word. Wellbeing is read by kind, day and number, never a note or an extra. Money is read only in the person's own currency. Gratitude is read only by which days have it. Supplements are never read. Nothing of it reaches the assistant, staff or the leaderboard. A coach gets it only through one consented door (see Life coaches, below). A line says things happen together and never that one causes another (`CAUSE_WORDS`, tested). "Not now" on a card is kept only on the device. It uses no model.
- **The leaderboard** lists people only after they opt in and finish setup. Nobody blocked either way with the viewer is listed. Journal and wellbeing never count. No date of birth or age band is ever used.
- **Anonymous posts, replies, event lines, channel lines and run discussion lines** show the reader a made-up name (`anonAlias`, hashed from the writer and the conversation). It is the same in one thread and different in the next. The reader never gets the id, handle, follower count or rank; staff keep the id so a report can be acted on. Circle members and Bond chats are aliases only, with no reveal, and member ids never reach the browser.
- **Life coaches:** a coach works only with students who said yes. What a coach sees is the student's choice per link, six switches (Daily and streaks, pillars and pyramids, routine and contract, life map answers, reflection, patterns). They are read fresh on every request, with the link active and the permission held at that moment. Never the journal, gratitude, a tracker's note, money, water, supplements, focus, time blocks, Discover, the Companion, practices, the contract's signed name or a review's snapshot. Every ask, answer, end, share change, note and task change is a line in `coach_log`, shown to both sides.
- **The coach's consented door for patterns (2026-11-07).** Emerging reaches a coach only through the sixth switch, Patterns. It reads off on every link made before it and on a link the team makes at once, since those students never saw the switch, and on for a link someone says yes to, since they see every switch first. `GET /coach/students/:id/patterns` runs the rules on read over facts built only from the parts that link shares, and the journal, gratitude, money, water, focus, time blocks, Discover, Agency and reviews' numbers are left empty, so the keep-list (`COACH_PATTERN_KEYS`) and the guard behind it are belts over braces. It is stored nowhere and uses no model. A coach may ask for the switch once a week, one inbox note, guarded on `patterns_asked_at` and logged. Suggestions from a number the student already shares stay when Patterns is off, because they say nothing the coach cannot read on the same page, and the switch says so. A line about eating is never made.
- **Adding a student.** The lookup box (`GET /coach/lookup`) shows only a picture, a name and a handle on an exact @username or friend code, never an email or a phone, and the same words for nobody and for someone blocked either way. An email is never looked up until it is sent, and someone who blocked the coach reads the same as an address with no account. **The invite link** (`coach_invites`) is kept plain while live, since the coach has to copy it, is never in the export, and is kept only as a hash once retired (a year, answering 410). Opening it says nothing about anyone but the coach, and nothing starts until the student taps Yes after seeing every switch.
- **Agency team members** work on the owner's Agency through one gate (`agencyTeamAccess.ts`) that reads their row on every request. Only routes on an allowlist open, at the tab's level the owner set (Off, View or Edit). The answer is scrubbed: no business profile or bank, private notes, fees, retainer amount, client link or Army links. A member's write is logged with who did it. A client member gets one read-only view.
- **Phone and date of birth** are never shown to anyone else; the date of birth cannot be changed.
- The smoke test (2,921 checks on 2026-11-10) proves much of this: journal, finance, not to do, the public page, Dev Tools, the staff checks, an owner's Agency records and templates, the business-metrics switch, and the client link's switches. Campaigns, applications and cohorts are checked by reading the code. Emerging's privacy was checked by hand on 2026-11-04: another account sees nothing of it. A coach's patterns are held by `coachReport.test.ts` and the Life coaches section of the smoke test.

## 3. Privacy as practised

- Sign-up stores an email and a password hash, plus the phone and date of birth above. City, area and interests are optional. A position is optional, rounded to three decimals, used only by the circle matcher and never returned.
- Click tracking on `/go`, `/out` and page events stores a salted hash of the address, never the address. `CLICK_SALT` is the salt (changing it changes the made-up names of anonymous posts too). The one optional cookie (`kl_click`) follows the consent rules in §14.
- KYC keeps a hash and the last four digits of PAN and bank numbers.
- The public page is opt-in part by part. A switched-off page returns only the name. The server enforces every switch, including friend requests and chats.
- Export is one JSON file (`GET /me/export`), and the delete is one function (`deleteAccount`). Since 2026-11-07 both cover every table that holds what a person wrote or what is about them, held by a scan (see §12). Erase wipes progress and keeps the account. Each export and delete is logged in `data_requests` with a kind and two times, no name.
- The privacy page (`/privacy`) says what is kept and for how long (a table read from `RETENTION` in shared), why, and on what ground. It names who handles the data (`/subprocessors`, read from `SUBPROCESSORS`) and the person's rights and how to use them. It covers the cookie, Global Privacy Control, the minimum age of 18 and AI. It leads with India: the DPDP Act 2023 ground beside the EU and UK one for each use, the data principal's rights including a nominee, the Grievance Officer (`BUSINESS_GRIEVANCE_NAME`, `_EMAIL`, `_PHONE`, shown as "not set yet" while empty) and then the Data Protection Board of India. The California notice sits under "If you live elsewhere". It has sections on Agency and your clients, the calendar link, life coaches, position, payments and KYC, the content strategist and the assistant; Discover is under "Who sees what".
- The terms page is a plain-language draft for a lawyer to finish. `LEGAL_VERSION` is 2026-11-10 (the waitlist and invite codes; before that 2026-11-07), since `/terms`, `/privacy` and `/subprocessors` changed that day; every account with an email sees the one-time notice once. Since 2026-11-05 it has "Wellbeing, not medical care", "Money tools, not advice" and a line under Acceptable use: posts and chat lines are their writers' views, and nothing may be posted to put down a religion, a community, a company or a person.
- No third-party analytics. Product events stay in our own table. Email goes through Resend only when a key is set, and the outbox keeps every message either way.

## 4. Safety and moderation as built

- Moderators hide posts and replies, and the author is told why. Guides (an admin, or a sub admin with the moderation permission) can pin posts and can switch on holding a new member's first post until a guide says okay (`hold_first_posts`, off by default).
- Anyone blocks anyone (`POST /bond/block`). It ends a friendship and hides posts, replies, event lines, channel lines and leaderboard rows both ways, and a blocked person cannot ask again. An introduction needs consent first, and a friend request from an event says which event it came from.
- Muting from Community is by rank (`COMMUNITY_RANKS`: member, guide, staff, admin). Only someone of a lower rank can be muted, and lifting a mute needs at least the muter's rank. Mutes apply in Community channels too.
- Circles and Bond chats use aliases only. There is no reveal.
- A line in a channel or a run discussion can be reported. The report becomes a help ticket with the line's words. A takedown by someone else is audited and wipes the words.
- Support tickets reach staff with the support permission.
- Random video chat is parked, as v0.1 required.
- Not built: a report button on posts, replies, circle messages, Bond chats, event lines or Store chats; a report queue of its own; a code of conduct page; appeals beyond writing to the team through Help.

## 5. The Companion's limits as built

- The module blurb says "Not a therapist." The screen's line says "Talk about your day. Not a therapist, and not for emergencies."
- The crisis reply (`CRISIS_LINE` in shared) says: call your local emergency number or go to the nearest hospital; in India, call Tele-MANAS on 14416 at any hour; tell a friend or someone in your family too. Help, the Companion's How it works and the journal prompts' safety line say "or go to the nearest hospital" and name Tele-MANAS 14416. A heavy journal prompt ends with a note to stop and talk with a professional or someone you trust.
- The public site's footer carries an emergency line: kreators.app cannot help in an emergency; call your local emergency number or go to the nearest hospital, tell someone you trust, and in India call Tele-MANAS on 14416.
- Crisis words are checked before any model call and before any credit is spent. They get the fixed crisis reply, and the words go to no model. A model's reply is checked again: if it touches a crisis and has not pointed to help, the crisis reply is added.
- **With a plan, credits and room in the day's cap,** the Companion answers through the assistant's model layer with the model the person picked (agent `companion`). It can only read, through a short list of tools (the day, the routine, reminders, the week plan, and the trackers, focus and the journal only for a model that keeps nothing). Any other tool name, a write included, is answered and never run. Mood, sleep and the wellbeing score reach only a model that keeps nothing. A model that may keep data never reads a past reply that was written from wellbeing.
- **Without a plan,** with no credits, with the cap used, or when the model fails before it says anything, the built-in Companion answers free. That is Workers AI on our own host (`@cf/meta/llama-3.1-8b-instruct`) when the binding is there, else fixed rules. It is never a paywall.
- The Workers AI prompt says: be warm, brief and concrete, and end with one question. Never diagnose or give medical advice. If someone mentions self-harm, tell them to contact local emergency services or a crisis line right away.
- Each Companion line records who answered (`chat_messages.model`: a model id, `workers-ai`, `local` or `crisis`), shown as "Answered by".
- The chat stays with the person and goes nowhere else.
- Wellbeing scores never rank anyone. They never reach a brand, staff, the leaderboard or Club selection, and never decide eligibility for anything.
- Not built: a way to report an unsafe reply. Crisis guidance names a number only for India; elsewhere it says the local emergency number and the nearest hospital. The text does not imply monitoring or emergency response, which is right.

## 6. The book collection

Nothing from the 10 TB collection or the 25,000 books is in the app. Knowledge is curated resources with their sources, seeded by migration and managed by staff. The founder's own three books are in the app as his own words (School courses, the Book page, draft Shop products); that is his own work, not the collection. `shubham.md` asks for a chatbot over the books and for the books in the Store. Before either, the v0.1 steps stand. Inventory the sources. Record author, publisher, source, licence and how each was acquired. Sort them into public domain, open licence, licensed and restricted. Decide the permitted uses. Exclude anything without a rights basis. Stop retrieval from returning long passages. Keep provenance and a takedown route. Owning a copy grants no right to train on it or distribute it.

## 7. AI data handling as built

Five things send text to a model. Each is optional and off without its key or binding.

- **The Companion** (§5). Without a plan it uses Workers AI (the optional `AI` binding). That gets the person's first name, streak, wellbeing score and label, today's mood, last night's sleep, the weakest pillar, the open task count, the next task title, the day's small act, and the recent chat. Cloudflare processes it inside the Worker's account. With a plan it goes through the assistant's endpoint, under the privacy rules below.
- **Course drafting** sends pasted notes to Workers AI.
- **The assistant** (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint from `ASSISTANT_BASE_URL`, OpenRouter by default, with `ASSISTANT_API_KEY`) runs only for a person on a plan. What they type, the chat so far and what a tool looks up go to the model; that can be their tasks and money. It acts as the person, on their own things, with the same limits as a tap. Every tool is a description over an existing route, run inside the Worker with the person's own Bearer token. So the session, the module switches, the rate limits, the waits, the xp caps and the owner filters apply exactly as to a tap. The user comes from the token, never from the model. Every id a model gives passes `seg` (no slash, no dot). The person's date and zone come from the device. A change is a row with Undo (`assistant_actions`: tool names, ids and plain labels, never journal text). Removing and settling wait for Confirm. A Stop button marks the message, and the loop checks before each model call and tool.
- **The feelings reader** ("Read with AI" on the strategy session's map card, §10) uses only a model that keeps nothing.
- **The content strategist** sends one brand folder at a time to Anthropic through the official SDK when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set, else to Workers AI when only the binding exists. It sends the brand's name, niche, description and voice, its sources with their summaries, the research runs the owner picked, and the brief. Nothing else about the person goes with it. Research goes out through Agent Reach, which is read-only and never posts or logs in for anyone; the bridge accepts only known channels and plain parameters.
- **Privacy follows the model.** A model listed as `private` has a zero-retention host and is asked to keep nothing (`ASSISTANT_ZDR`). Only then are the journal and tracker reads, and a Discover summary, offered. The journal is read only when the person asks about it. A model that keeps data is sent without that ask and never offered those reads; the panel says so under the picker. A model off the list is asked for zero retention unless `ASSISTANT_ZDR` is "off".
- Journal text is never sent to a model, except by the assistant or the Companion with a plan, only for its owner, and only through a `private` model; the assistant reads it only when asked. Rules, not models, run Discover's next steps, Emerging, a coach's patterns and suggestions, Plan my week, plan strength, the block hints, the daily brief, the reminders and "Read my words".
- The system prompts hold no secret and no privileged logic. A model's reply is drawn as small markdown with no links and no HTML.
- `/subprocessors` names Anthropic (Content), OpenRouter and its model hosts (the assistant and the Companion with a plan), and Cloudflare Workers AI (drafts, and the Companion without a plan), each with what it gets.
- **Never trained on.** The terms and the privacy page both say: we never use what you write to train AI models, and the companies that run the assistant or the writing tool get only what the job needs. That holds for the app's own code: nothing is exported for training. The assistant's panel says which models keep data, and a model that keeps data never receives the journal or a tracker.

## 8. Minors

The minimum age is 18. Sign-up asks for a date of birth and the server refuses anyone younger; the terms say the same. There is no guardian flow and no age verification beyond the date typed. So the rule keeps honest people out and relies on the terms for the rest.

## 9. Incident response

`docs/security/SECURITY_RUNBOOK.md` is the written plan for the person on call (today the founder): where to look, how to find one person's activity, and what to do in order. `RESTORE.md` says how to restore the database. The tools: `pnpm cf:logs` for live logs, Wrangler observability, the app's own log (Admin, Logs), the audit log, the webhook log with replay, the email outbox, `pnpm cf:secret` to rotate a key, and D1's own point-in-time recovery (30 days). `SECRETS.md` lists every secret and how to rotate it. ## 10. Agency, the client link and the private calendar link

Agency is owner only: every query filters by the session user id, and the whole app is behind its module gate. What leaves the account does so through a link with a secret in it, each a switch the owner controls. Token secrets come from a 32-letter alphabet, so 16 characters are about 80 bits.

- **The client link** (`/a/<token>`, 16 characters) shows one client's dashboard, only the parts the owner switched on. Waiting for approval, coming up, published, progress and shared notes are on by default; channels, hours, invoices and agreements off. `GET /public/agency/:token` builds only what is on; what is off is never sent. Never in it, whatever is switched on: the owner's private notes, per-piece fees, the retainer amount (except inside a retainer offer the owner sent), time entries line by line, other clients, and what a brand wrote in Army. Approving, sending back and commenting can each be switched off and are refused with 403. All writes are limited per address (`portal`). A fresh link closes the old one; switching it off closes it.
- **The password on a client link** is the owner's, per client. A new client cannot be added without one (`POST /agency/clients` answers 400). It is hashed with sign-in's own PBKDF2 and compared in constant time, with a decoy hash when the link is unknown so the time matches. A wrong password and an unknown link get the same words. Unlocking is limited per address (`portalLogin`) and per client (`portalLoginLink`). It gives a portal session: 32 random bytes, kept as a SHA-256 hash in `portal_sessions`, for twelve hours. The browser holds it in sessionStorage only, never a cookie or localStorage. The same session opens that client's agreement, invoice and document links through one gate (`requirePortalAccess`, `portalDocGate`). Changing or clearing the password, a fresh link or closing it ends every session. Hashes and sessions are never in the export. Only the owner sets, changes, generates or clears it; no client route can.
- **Asking for a new password.** A client's lock screen offers "Ask for a new password" with an optional short reason (limit `portalPasswordAsk`). It answers the same for an unknown link, a link with no password and one already asked about. One ask is open per client. The reason is kept in that row only, never in a log. The owner is told in the inbox and by one private email. The app never sends a password; the owner shares the new one another way.
- **Client onboarding.** A client added since 2026-11-03 sees only a short form after unlocking, until they send it once. It asks the company's legal name, address, optional GSTIN and PAN, contact, billing email and the trial brief. The details land on the client record. The owner's email about it never carries the answers.
- **The team link** (`/t/<token>`, 16 characters) is a second link per client for the client's own team. It shows the work only: pieces to review, coming up, published, progress, notes and channels. Approving from it is off until the owner allows it. It has its own token, switches, password, sessions (`team_link_sessions`) and password ask. It cannot be switched on without a password, and its password cannot be removed while it is on. Invoices, money, agreements, other documents, the retainer, onboarding, billing, hours and private notes are never on it (`TEAM_LINK_NEVER`). The page has no field for them, and its routes find clients only by the team token. Every client link, agreement and invoice route answers 404 to a request that carries a team session (`refuseTeamSession`). Renewing or closing one link never touches the other.
- **An invoice link** (`/i/<token>`, 12 characters, about 60 bits, works only once the invoice is not a draft) carries the invoice with the owner's switches applied on the server before anything is sent (`invoiceForClient`). The bank and UPI details, tax numbers, due date and notes can each be hidden, and the client's browser never receives what is off. Comments and the download are switches too. The client's own Agency client id never leaves.
- **An agreement link** (`/k/<token>`, 16 characters) and a document link (`/d/<token>`, 16 characters) show the paper as sent. The client answers the brief (only answers to questions that exist and options that were offered are kept) and accepts by typing their name. That is typed acceptance, not a certified e-signature, and the screen says so. A signed agreement is never edited. Comments are a switch. Closing it (taking it back to a draft, or voiding it) closes the link; a restored void gets its old link back.
- **The Agency team.** The owner asks a member by email, @username or friend code (limit `teamInvite`). A block reads like an unknown person. Each member gets Off, View or Edit per tab. A member never reaches settings, adding or deleting clients, the client link or its password, Army, time, template writes or the team itself (§2). Both sides are told in the inbox. The rows are in both people's export and go with either account.
- **The private calendar link** (`/api/public/calendar/<token>.ics`, 24 characters, about 120 bits) is off until the person switches it on. It carries the title, the pillar and the priority of each open task that has a day (and its time in the person's zone), and the name and time of the reviews they scheduled. It never carries a note, the journal, a mood or anything from a tracker. It is one way: it shows tasks in a calendar and never changes them. A fresh link closes the old one.
- **Personal details:** the business profile (sender, tax numbers, bank, signer) is saved on the person's own account. It is shown only on an invoice or an agreement they choose to send, and only the parts they leave switched on.
- **Business metrics** in Reviews are refused by the server (403) until the person switches them on.
- Token secrets are random (`randomCode`), never guessable from an id, never listed anywhere. A link that does not exist answers 404 with no detail. A link the owner renewed or closed answers 410 `closed` with the business's name, logo and email only (`retired_portal_links`, a SHA-256 of the old token, never the token). The locked answer (401 `locked`) carries the client's name and the business's name and logo (`agencyFace`), nothing else.
- **The feelings reader** ("Read with AI" on the strategy session's map card, `POST /assistant/feelings`) needs a plan and a model that keeps nothing (`private`). The words are sent once and never stored or logged; the ledger keeps only the tokens and the model. "Read my words" stays on the page, rules only.

## 11. Logs, errors and retention

The app's own log (`app_logs`, `apps/api/src/logs.ts`) has a line for every change anyone makes (any request that is not a plain read). It also logs every refusal (4xx, rate limits, failed sign-ins), every server error with its message, every cron run that did something, and every error a browser reports (`POST /public/client-error`, limited per address). A line holds the area, the cleaned path (ids replaced), the status, the time it took and who did it; never a body or a query string. A client or team link appears as `:token`. Nothing from the journal, wellbeing, Companion, Discover or the assistant is logged but a bare server-error line with no person and no path. Only a super admin (role `admin`) reads it, since it names people. Lines are kept thirty days (`KEEP_DAYS`; the cron prunes), and a deleted account's name is wiped from them. Observability counts (including Agency's) are counts only: no names, amounts or notes.

## 12. Erase, delete and export

- **What the two promise (2026-11-07).** The export and the delete cover every table that holds what a person wrote or what is about them. `scripts/data-coverage.mjs` holds one line for each table with a column naming a person: its export key and what the delete does. `scan.sh` checks each line against `routes/me.ts` and `account.ts`, and a new table fails the scan until someone decides. The cross-check that made it went through all 224 tables and added 77 to the export (202 keys) and 66 to the delete. The smoke check "Export and delete cover what a person wrote" searches every column of every table afterwards. `node scripts/data-coverage.mjs --table` prints the whole table.
- **Export** (`GET /me/export`): one JSON file, table by table. It never holds a hash, a salt or a session, nor a code someone else could use (a host's code, the calendar token, a coach's invite link). Mail is by subject only, and clicks are counts.
- **Erase** (`POST /me/reset`, under Profile) wipes progress but keeps the device session so the person can start over. It clears pyramids, not to do, reviews, xp and scores, the strategy session, Discover, the assistant's actions and the points ledger. It keeps what must not be undone or is not progress: a plan and its credits, unlocked card games, attendee claims and run entries (clearing them would let a code or a run pay again), a life coach and their links, the person's business records (Agency, invoices), the journal, gratitude and the seen marks, every tracker with Fitness and the supplement list, and time blocks. The founder confirmed on 2026-11-07 that an erase keeps gratitude and Fitness logs.
- **Delete** (the person's own, or an admin's from People, never an admin account or one's own; both through `deleteAccount` in `apps/api/src/account.ts`, audited without the name). Account deletion needs the word DELETE. The rule: what is private goes; what other people or the books rely on stays with the name and words taken out, under the account row called "Deleted member", which keeps only its id. So a review a person wrote goes (no rating is stored, so a product's average stays true to the reviews left). Leads go with the account that gathered them, and a lead holding the person's own address goes too. An event, a run or a session still to come is cancelled and whoever was coming told (ticket points back), and the buyer of a paid question still open is told. An order, a booking, an invoice, a paid reward entry, an approved pitch, a campaign role and a past event with guests stay without their words. What the team made (a program, published knowledge, a verified tool) stays without who made it. It runs fifty statements to a round trip, each safe to run twice, with the sessions and the account row last, so a run that stops halfway can be asked for again. `tidyOld` also clears notes written to a deleted account afterwards.
- **What a delete leaves, on purpose.** A note already in someone else's inbox keeps the words it was written with, since it names no sender and cannot be found. A help ticket made from a report quotes the reported line and stays with the reporter. `circle_groups.plan` and `venue` have no author recorded. `bond_chats` stays for the other person, with made-up names only. What the founder still has to decide is in 01 §12, Still open.

## 13. Hardening from the 2026-11-01 sweep

Four read-only reviewers went over the shared rules, the server, the newer server features and the screens; each proved a bug before it was reported. What was fixed and is now the rule:

- **A typed key finds nothing it should not.** Every `*_BY_KEY` lookup in shared is built by `byKey` as a null-prototype object. So a key like `constructor` or `toString` finds nothing instead of an inherited function (it used to answer 500).
- **A date must be a real day.** `DATE_RE.test` in `apps/api/src/db.ts` checks the calendar, not only the shape: `2026-11-31` and `2026-00-00` are refused with a plain error, never a 500. `parseRepeat` drops an end day that is not a real day. `GET /wellbeing/:kind?days=abc` no longer fails.
- **Members-only community posts stay members-only.** Liking, reading the replies and replying all go through `canSeePost` (the owner or a buyer of the membership); anyone else gets 404.
- **Roles.** `POST /admin/users/:id/role` and the same change in Dev Tools refuse changing your own role (400). Adding a teammate to an organization is `INSERT OR IGNORE`, so it cannot demote an owner who is already a member.
- **A coach cannot set a due day before the student's do day** (`DATES_PROBLEM`), the same rule the student's own edit has.
- **Links.** `parseLinkBlocks` refuses an https link that holds control characters and gives every block its own id. The calendar file escapes a lone carriage return and folds lines before 75 bytes without splitting a character.
- **Free and double use closed.** The test payment path cannot make a points-only product or a points-priced program free, and points and refunds move with a guard first (see 07 §16).

## 14. State after the 2026-11-01 security, privacy and performance pass

Three launch checklists (legal, security, performance) were worked through with the rule that nothing may break. Every change was proved by the full smoke suite (910 checks at the time), the browser drivers, the layout probes and a 102-route Chrome browse for policy violations. `docs/security/SECURITY_REPORT.md` is the table of the 27 checks and 7 bonus checks, each with its status (Passed, Fixed and re-tested, or Open), evidence and notes; this section is the summary. Nothing was run against production.

**Headers and browser rules.**
- One enforcing Content-Security-Policy, written in code (`CSP` in `apps/api/src/headers.ts`) and in `apps/web/public/_headers` (the smoke test checks that the two match). It allows the own origin for scripts, styles, fonts and connections, plus Razorpay's checkout hosts for the payment window. `frame-src` is only YouTube (no-cookie), Vimeo and Razorpay. It sets `object-src 'none'` and `frame-ancestors 'none'`. Inline styles are allowed (React style attributes). It was first shipped as report-only. `scripts/smoke/csp-browse.mjs` over 102 routes found three needs, now in the policy, and then 0 violations. The browse was run again over 118 pages on 2026-11-03. Going back to watch-only is one word in two files. Both fonts (Google Sans Flex and Caveat) are self-hosted, so `font-src 'self'` holds and no third party sees a visitor on load.
- HSTS for two years (`max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains`, no preload), `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` (`no-referrer` on the reset page), `Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=(self)`, `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin-allow-popups`.
- **No CORS.** `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` was on every API answer and is gone. A state-changing request with a foreign `Origin` header is refused with 403 `wrong origin`. No `Origin` (scripts, webhooks) passes, as do the site's own host, `PUBLIC_URL` and loopback in development. Webhooks are checked by signature, not origin.
- Every API answer carries `X-Request-Id`; an authenticated answer is `Cache-Control: no-store` (Explore and uploaded pictures set their own, see `cachecontrol.ts`). A 5xx answers `{ error, requestId }` and nothing else. A malformed JSON body is a 400, and its parser text, which can quote a password, is neither returned nor logged.
- **Blocked paths.** Hidden files, backups, dumps, config, source, logs and `.map` files answer 404 from the Worker (`blockedPath` in `headers.ts` and the `run_worker_first` list in `wrangler.jsonc`, kept in step). The app shell used to answer 200. `sourcemap: false` in the build. `bash scripts/check-exposed.sh <domain>` asks for them and fails on any 200.

**Outbound requests and links.**
- `apps/api/src/safeurl.ts` (`publicHttpsUrl`, `safeFetch`) guards every fetch of an address a person influenced (`reach.ts`). It allows https only, with no IP numbers, localhost, internal names, logins or odd ports; at most 3 redirects, each re-checked; a timeout and a size cap. A public name that resolves to a private address is not caught (no DNS in a Worker; a Worker cannot reach private networks anyway).
- `httpUrl` in `db.ts` keeps only `http` or `https` addresses typed into meeting, replay, video and event link fields (Shop products and lessons, School sessions, Party and Admin events); `javascript:` and the like were dropped on the server at first. Since 2026-11-07 a bad link is refused with a 400 and the rule is wider (§1).

**Alerts, sign-in and mail.**
- `apps/api/src/alerts.ts`, run by the fifteen-minute cron, tells every admin in the inbox (Team tab), once an hour per kind. It fires on 20 failed sign-ins, 100 answers of 429, 20 server errors, or any role or permission change in fifteen minutes. The text holds counts only. A plain read that takes 1.5 seconds or more is logged as a warning (`SLOW_READ_MS`). **Alert emails (2026-11-07):** each new note also goes out as one private email, tied to the note, so the once an hour is the note's.
