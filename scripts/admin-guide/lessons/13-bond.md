# Bond: friends, codes, chats and blocks
Riya met an editor called Arjun at a meetup. They want to swap tips, maybe share a client. Bond lets them become friends, talk, and stay safe while they do. Nothing moves in Bond until both people say yes.

In this lesson you'll learn:

- How usernames and friend codes work.
- How Riya adds a friend and answers a request.
- How anonymous chats and private channels differ.
- How introductions after an event work.
- How blocks work, and what an admin can check.

## Switch it on

Bond is off on a new account. Riya opens **Apps**, taps the **Connection** group and turns on the switch beside **Bond**.

## Usernames and friend codes

Every account has two things that find it.

- **Username:** her page address, like @riya_edits. A new account gets one that starts with user_ and a few letters, so it is never blank. She changes it in the **Public page** app, in the **Username** field. It takes three to twenty letters, numbers or underscores, and nobody else can have it.
- **Friend code:** eight letters and numbers, shown on Bond. It is only for adding friends.

## Step by step: adding Arjun

1. Riya opens **Bond** and scrolls to **Friends**.
2. Under **Your friend code** she taps **Copy** and sends the code to Arjun.
3. Arjun opens Bond, types it in **Add a friend by code** and taps **Send request**.
4. On Riya's Bond, a card says "Arjun would like to be friends". She taps **Yes**.
5. Arjun now shows in her list as **Friends**, with a **Chat** button.

There is a second way. Riya opens someone's public page and taps **Add friend**. If they asked her first, the button reads **Accept request**. A request she sent shows as "Request sent, waiting" with **Cancel**. **Remove** ends a friendship. Both ask first in a sheet.

## Chats under aliases

A chat in Bond always runs under two made-up names. Nobody's real name moves through it, even between friends.

1. Riya taps **Chat** beside Arjun.
2. The chat opens under her alias and his.
3. She writes and sends. The chat shows under **Chats** at the top of Bond.

A chat can also start from someone at her circle table, or from a person's page with **Chat anonymously**. There is no reveal button. That is the rule.

## Channels for friends

A channel is different: a small private room where names show.

1. Under **Channels**, Riya taps **New channel**.
2. She names it "Pune editors" and taps **Make the channel**.
3. Inside it she taps **Ask friends** and taps **Ask** beside Arjun and two others.
4. Each of them sees "You were asked into this channel" with **Say yes** or **No thanks**.

Only accepted friends can be asked. A room holds up to twelve people, counting the ones asked, and Riya can own up to five rooms. As the owner she can take someone out with the small cross by their name, take down any line, and **Close channel**. Anyone else can **Leave**. A person not in the room cannot open it at all. Nobody is anonymous in a Bond channel.

## Introductions after an event

Bond also helps people meet the ones they crossed paths with.

1. When Riya signs up for a Party event, she ticks **Introduce me to other guests**.
2. On Bond, under **Introductions**, she turns on **Open to introductions**.
3. People from her events who said yes show under **People you met**.
4. She taps **Introduce** beside one, writes **Why you would like to meet**, and taps **Send the request**.
5. They see her name and her note, and tap **Yes, introduce us** or **No thanks**.

Her details only move after the other person says yes.

## Blocks

Riya can block anyone, any time. She finds **Block** in many places: a post's **More** menu in Community, a channel line's **More**, a reply, or an introduction card.

A block works both ways:

- **Friendship:** it ends.
- **Posts and replies:** neither sees the other's.
- **Event and channel lines:** hidden both ways.
- **The leaderboard:** neither shows on the other's board.
- **Asks:** neither can send the other a friend request or an introduction.

The blocked person is not told. Blocking from an introduction offers a **Reason, for the team only** box. Riya can lift a block any time under **People you blocked** with **Unblock**, which asks first.

## Her page's switches

On the **Public page** app, under **What is public**, two switches shape Bond:

- **Let people add me as a friend:** off hides the Add friend button.
- **Let friends start a chat:** off means nobody can open a chat with her.

The server enforces both, not only the screen.

> **Pro tip:** tell new members to share their friend code, not their phone number. A code adds a friend and gives nothing else away.

> **Result:** Riya has Arjun as a friend, a private room with three editors, and a chat that never shows her name. She knows she can block anyone in one tap.

## What the admin checks

- **Anonymity:** chats and circle members are aliases only. Member ids never reach the browser, and there is no reveal, for you either.
- **Deleted accounts:** a deleted member never shows in friends or requests.
- **Switches:** in **Admin**, **Flags**, the Bond row sets **On**, **Minimum role**, **Rollout %** and **Overrides**. In **People**, tap a row, then **What does this person see?** to set it for one person.
- **Reports:** a reported channel line becomes a help ticket with the line's words, in the support queue.
- **Public page:** the friend and chat switches are the member's own choice. Do not ask them to turn them on.

If someone says they cannot add a friend, check three things: Bond is on for both, the other person allows friend requests, and neither has blocked the other. A block reads the same as an unknown person, on purpose.

## The bottom line

Bond is built on consent. Friends need a yes, introductions need a yes, and channels need a yes. Teach members to use friend codes and to block without guilt. As an admin, protect the aliases and leave the privacy switches to each person.
