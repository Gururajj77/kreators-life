# Signing up and the setup chat
Riya edits videos in Pune. She posts her own Reels, and she edits for brands on the side. Today she opens kreators.app for the first time. In a few taps the app should know who she is and show what fits her.

In this lesson you'll learn:

- How a new person gets from Welcome to Home.
- What the account card and the privacy card ask, and why.
- How devices and passwords work.
- What you, as an admin, can check and change.

## Step one: Welcome

Riya taps Start free on the site. That opens Welcome. It says "Seven relationships. One you." and shows one big button.

While **Invite only** is on, Welcome asks for an invite code first. Riya's friend on the team sent her an invite link, so her code is already in and Welcome works as below. Someone with no code types one in, or taps **Join the waitlist** and leaves a name and email. Each code makes one account, once.

1. She taps **Start with a short chat**.
2. The app opens a session on her phone, so nothing she says is lost.
3. The setup chat opens with Bumling, the app's own guide.

If she stops halfway, the button says **Carry on setting up** next time. The line under it says her answers are saved on this device.

## Step two: the account card

Bumling asks for the account first. The card has four fields.

1. **Your name:** Riya types "Riya".
2. **Email:** her work email.
3. **Password:** eight or more letters, numbers or signs. It cannot be her email or a common one.
4. **Password again:** the same one, so a typo cannot lock her out.

She taps **Next**. The eye button inside each password field shows or hides what she typed.

## Step three: the private card

Next comes a second, small card with two private details.

- **Phone:** she types her ten-digit number. A number with no country code is read as Indian and gets +91.
- **Date of birth:** you must be 18 or older to join. Nobody else ever sees it.
- **The terms box:** "I agree to the Terms of use and have read the Privacy policy." It starts unticked. She must tick it herself.

She taps **Make my account**. The chat says she is in.

## Step four: the few questions

Now Bumling asks a few quick questions. Each list comes in small groups of chips. Riya taps what fits and then **Next**.

1. **What she wants:** up to three goals. Riya picks "Earn from my content", "Manage my clients and invoices" and "Plan my week and stay on track".
2. **Who she is:** she picks "I make content" and "I freelance for clients".
3. **What she makes:** since she does both, she gets two groups, "Your own content" and "For clients". She picks "Reels and Shorts" and "Editing".
4. **What gets in the way:** she picks "Not enough time".

After each answer, Bumling says it back in plain words. Under every question sit **Tell me more** and **Change my last answer**. The typing box works at any time. Riya types "what is Army?" and gets a plain answer. Then her choices come back.

## Step five: the apps

Bumling shows a short list of apps, each with one line. Since Riya said time is tight, the list is short on purpose. **Why these apps?** shows the reason for each one. She could untick any app she does not want. She keeps all three and taps **Show 3 apps**. Every other app the chat can show or hide is hidden, and she can show any of them later.

The last card offers a few next steps: **Go to Home**, the strategy session, the Discover quiz, and **Add my interests**. Riya picks Go to Home.

> **Pro tip:** tell new people they can run this again any time. On the Profile tab, **Match my apps again** opens the same chat with their last answers filled in.

## Step six: the privacy card

A small card called **Your privacy** sits at the bottom left. It asks about three things, and all three start off.

- **Remember which link brought me:** a cookie for thirty days that tells a creator which tracked link sent you.
- **Show videos from YouTube and Vimeo:** off means a video waits for a tap.
- **Send me news and offers by email:** off means only the mail you need, like receipts.

Riya can tap **Reject all** or **Accept all**. They look the same, so neither is pushed. **Choose for each** opens the three switches and a **Save my choices** button. She can change them later on the Profile tab, under Privacy choices.

## Devices and passwords

- **Three devices at once:** an account can be signed in on three devices. A fourth signs out the oldest one. That device's sign-in screen then says why.
- **Change password:** on the Profile tab, under Account, Riya taps **Change password**. She types the current one, then the new one twice. Every other device is signed out.
- **Forgot it:** the sign-in screen has **Forgot your password?** It mails a one-time link that works for thirty minutes.

> **Result:** Riya has an account, her tags, her apps and her privacy choices. Home is open.

## What an admin checks

Open **Admin** from the Operations group on Apps. Only admins see it.

1. Go to the **People** tab. Search by name, email, page or id.
2. Find Riya's row. You see her role, plan, credits, xp and points. You never see her password, phone or date of birth.
3. Tap her row. You can give permissions, honors, points, xp, badges and a plan.
4. Under **What does this person see?** tick or untick an app for her alone. **Reset** brings the usual rules back.
5. A wrong sign-up can be removed with the delete icon at the end of the row. It asks first in a sheet. An admin account, or your own, cannot be deleted there.

A few more things to know:

- **Founder emails:** an address on the admin list gets the admin role at sign-up, and needs no invite code. Sign up with yours before anyone else can.
- **Invites:** the **Invites** tab holds the waitlist, the codes and the **Invite only** switch. Admin: People walks through it.
- **Alerts:** many failed sign-ins in a short time send a note to every admin's inbox, at most once an hour.
- **Logs:** a super admin can read refusals and failed sign-ins under Logs. No message bodies are kept there.
- **Audit:** every role change and hand-given xp shows on the **Audit** tab.

## The bottom line

Sign-up is one chat. Two small cards make the account, a few taps pick the apps, and privacy starts off. As an admin, you check people in People and leave their private details alone.
