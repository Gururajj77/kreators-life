# Reviews: look back by period
You worked hard last month. But did you move the things that matter, or only the loud ones? Days blur together. A review stops the blur. The app brings the numbers from every app you use. You bring the words.

In this lesson you'll learn:

- How the four periods work, and how to write your first review.
- How templates give a review your own questions.
- How the review calendar reminds you on the day.
- How to bring a task to a review, so nothing you meant to think about gets lost.

## Why reviews are there

Daily is about today. Your pillars are about the long run. A review is the bridge between them. It asks a few plain questions once a week, a month, a quarter or half a year. Over time those answers show you how you work, not just what you did.

## Switch it on

Reviews start off on a new account. Open **Apps**, tap the **Reflection** group and turn on the switch beside **Reviews**.

## The four periods

A review covers one period:

- **Week:** Monday to Sunday. The built-in one is the **Weekly review**.
- **Month:** a calendar month, the **Monthly review**.
- **Quarter:** three months, the **Quarterly review**.
- **Half year:** six months, the **Mid-year check-in**.

You get one review per period. A period that has not started yet cannot be reviewed. Use the period control near the top to pick the kind, then the arrows to step back or forward.

## Your first review

Say you want to look back on last month.

1. Use the period control at the top. Pick **Month**, then step back to last month.
2. Under **The numbers**, each tile sits next to the month before. Tap one to go where it comes from.
3. **Day by day** shows your xp each day, and where it came from by pillar.
4. Under **Your review**, answer the questions. A few lines each is plenty.
5. Under **How did it go?**, tap a face.
6. Tap **Save this review**.

The Monthly review asks things like "What are you proud of this month?" and "What will you change next month?". The Weekly review asks "What went well this week?" and "What got in the way?".

Your review is saved with the numbers as they were that day. New data later never rewrites it. You can come back, change your words and **Save changes**. Saved reviews show under **Past reviews**. Deleting one asks first.

## Your own templates

The built-in questions always stay. You can add your own beside them.

1. Under **Templates**, tap **New template**.
2. Give it a **Name**, like "Money month".
3. In **Comes back every**, pick the period.
4. Under **Questions, one per line**, write your questions.
5. Tap **Add template**.

Next time, **Questions from** lets you choose the built-in set or yours. You can keep up to twelve templates.

## The review calendar

A review you forget to do is a review you skip. The calendar fixes that.

1. Under **Review calendar**, tap **Schedule a review**, or tap a day on the calendar.
2. Under **Which review**, pick the period.
3. Set the **First day** and the **Time**, and a **Name** if you like.
4. Under **Repeat**, pick how often: Every day, Weekdays, Every week, Every month or Every year, with an end day if you want one.
5. Tap **Schedule it**.

On that day, at that time, one note lands in your Inbox. If the day is the first of a period, the note opens the one that just ended, so a review on the 1st opens last month. On the calendar, a bell marks a reminder and a tick marks a review you wrote. Each reminder has a **Remind me** switch. You can schedule up to twenty.

> **Pro tip:** schedule the Weekly review for Sunday evening and the Monthly review for the first of the month. The note opens the right period, so starting takes one tap.

## Bring a task to the review

Some tasks are worth thinking about, not just ticking. Say you have "Raise my rates" and want to weigh it at month end.

1. On Daily, open the task's plan.
2. Turn on **Bring it to my review**. The task now wears a **For review** tag.
3. In that period's review, it shows under **Tasks you brought**, done or still open.
4. Tap **Add to my answer**. It adds a line to your answer and never wipes what you wrote.

A repeating task carries the flag to the next one.

## Business metrics

If you run your own business, **Business metrics** lets you track a few numbers of your own against a target each period. It stays off until you switch it on, so most people never see it.

## Who sees your reviews

Your reviews are yours. If you work with a life coach, they see saved reviews only while you leave that switch on for them, and never the numbers saved with it.

## What emerges over time

After a few weeks your answers start to repeat. The same thing "got in the way" three weeks running. That is a pattern worth acting on. Emerging, covered in a later lesson, may also show how this period compares with the one since your last review.

## What develops

You get better at telling busy from useful. You start to plan the next week from what the last one taught you. Small turns each week add up to a clear change by the half year.

> **Result:** a review for every period, your own questions, a note on the day, and the tasks you flagged waiting for you.

## The bottom line

Pick a period, answer a few questions, and schedule the next one before you close the page. A few minutes a week of looking back makes the next week better.
