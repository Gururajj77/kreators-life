# Reviews: look back by period
You worked hard last month. But did you move the things that matter, or only the loud ones? Days blur together. A review stops the blur. The app brings the numbers from every app you use, and one layer of your own pyramids. You bring the words.

In this lesson you'll learn:

- How the six periods work, and which layer of your pyramids each one walks.
- How to write a review, one question at a time.
- How templates give a review your own questions.
- How the review calendar reminds you on the day.
- How to bring a task to a review, so nothing you meant to think about gets lost.

## Why reviews are there

Daily is about today. Your pillars are about the long run. A review is the bridge between them. It asks a few plain questions about one stretch of time. Over the months those answers show you how you work, not just what you did.

## Find it

Reviews starts hidden on a new account, unless the setup chat showed it for you. Open **Apps**, open the **Reflection** group, open **Hidden apps** at its end and tap the eye beside **Reviews**.

## The six periods

A review covers one period, and each period walks one layer of your pyramids, pillar by pillar.

- **Day:** the **Daily review** walks your tasks.
- **Week:** Monday to Sunday. The **Weekly review** walks your projects.
- **Month:** a calendar month. The **Monthly review** walks your goal outcomes.
- **Quarter:** three months. The **Quarterly review** walks your value goals.
- **Half year:** six months, and it walks your guiding principles in two parts. The **Mid-year check-in** opens in July. The **Start-of-year setting** opens in January, beside the yearly review.
- **Year:** the **Yearly review** walks your foundations, what held and what comes next.

You get one review per period. A period that has not started yet cannot be reviewed. Use the period control near the top to pick the kind, then the arrows to step back or forward. Picking Half year or Year opens the one whose end is nearer.

## What is on the page

Say you want to look back on this week. From the top, the page goes like this.

1. **The review calendar:** what is coming up, and what you wrote.
2. **The numbers:** each tile sits next to the week before. Tap one to go where it comes from. Water, habits and more sit here too, and focus and budgets show while Mindfulness and Finance are on.
3. **Your projects, this week:** the layer this review walks, pillar by pillar. **Add to my answer** puts a project's line under the first question.
4. **Tasks you brought:** any task you flagged, done or still open.
5. **Your weekly review:** the questions, one at a time.

**See what's emerging**, beside The numbers, opens Emerging on the same period.

## Your first review

Under **Your weekly review**, one question shows at a time. The numbers along the top let you jump back to any of them.

1. Answer the question. A few lines is plenty.
2. Read the small line under it. It names the idea behind the question, such as CBT, ACT, improv or the Gita.
3. Tap **Next** until you reach **Rate it**.
4. Under **How did it go?**, tap a face, and write **The one thing to remember** in a line.
5. Tap **Next: save**, then **Save this review**.

The Weekly review opens with "Go through your projects, pillar by pillar. Which ones moved this week, and what moved them?" The Monthly review asks "Which results came from your effort, and which came from luck or other people?"

A review is a look back, not therapy. Skip any question that feels heavy. If something stays heavy, talk it through with a professional or someone you trust.

Your review is saved with the numbers as they were that day. New data later never rewrites it. You can come back, change your words and **Save changes**. Saved reviews show under **Past reviews**, and daily ones fold apart under **Daily reviews**. Deleting one asks first.

## Your own templates

Every period has one built-in set of questions, and it always stays. You can read them all under **Templates**, in **Questions for each period**.

- **Make my own copy** copies a built-in set so you can change it.
- **New template** starts from nothing. Give it a **Name**, like "Money month". In **Comes back every**, pick the period. Under **Questions, one per line**, write your questions. Then tap **Add template**.

Next time, **Questions from** lets you choose the built-in set or yours. You can keep up to twelve templates.

## The review calendar

A review you forget to do is a review you skip. The calendar fixes that.

1. Tap **Schedule a review**, or tap a day on the calendar.
2. Under **Which review**, pick the period.
3. Set the **First day** and the **Time**, and a name if you like.
4. Under **Repeat**, pick how often: Every day, Weekdays, Every week, Every month or Every year, with an end day if you want one.
5. Tap **Schedule it**.

On that day, at that time, one note lands in your Inbox. If the day is the first of a period, the note opens the one that just ended, so a review on the 1st opens last month. A half year or a year goes by the nearer end, so a yearly note on 5 January opens the year just gone.

On the calendar, a bell marks a reminder and a tick marks a review you wrote. A daily review takes a dot instead, and rings no bell, so a month of them stays calm. Each reminder has a **Remind me** switch. Removing one takes a second tap, and the reviews you wrote stay. You can schedule up to twenty.

> **Pro tip:** schedule the Weekly review for Sunday evening and the Monthly review for the first of the month. The note opens the right period, so starting takes one tap.

## Bring a task to the review

Some tasks are worth thinking about, not just ticking. Say you have "Raise my rates" and want to weigh it at month end.

1. On Daily, open the task's plan.
2. Turn on **Bring it to my review**. The task now wears a **For review** tag.
3. In that period's review, it shows under **Tasks you brought**, done or still open.
4. Tap **Add to my answer**. It adds a line to your answer and never wipes what you wrote.

A repeating task carries the flag to the next one.

## Business metrics

If you run your own business, **Business metrics** lets you track a few numbers of your own against a target each period. It stays off until you switch it on, so most people never see it. It works from the week to the year, since a day is too short for a business number.

## Who sees your reviews

Your reviews are yours. If you work with a life coach, they see saved reviews only while you leave that switch on for them, and never the numbers saved with it.

## What emerges over time

After a few weeks your answers start to repeat. The same project got stuck three weeks running. That is a pattern worth acting on. Emerging, covered in a later lesson, may also show how this period compares with the one since your last review.

## What develops

You get better at telling busy from useful. You start to plan the next week from what the last one taught you. Small turns each week add up to a clear change by the half year.

> **Result:** a review for every period, each walking one layer of your pyramids, your own questions if you want them, a note on the day, and the tasks you flagged waiting for you.

## The bottom line

Pick a period, answer a few questions, and schedule the next one before you close the page. A few minutes a week of looking back makes the next week better.
